# House opinion playbook (58 past markets)

How the team's analysts turn qualitative factors into numbers. Reasoning habits with typical magnitudes - not targets. 'house habit' = how sheets are built rather than how markets behave.

## 2A - country totals, regional split, growth

**Method.** At stage 2A the analyst should work in this order: (1) anchor the base year using the hardest primary source available—regulated quota, official trade data, company product revenue, or a credible third-party supply outlook—and cross-check with 2–3 independent demand indicators; if no direct source exists, use a proxy chain and calibrate to the historical CAGR. (2) Set forecast_growth_vs_history relative to history, using a default premium of +0.5 to +0.9pp for mature markets with structural tailwinds, +0.9 to +1.2pp for early-growth or technology-adoption markets, and 0.0 to +0.3pp for saturated markets. (3) Set region_split by first choosing the dominant demand type: manufacturing/industrial, healthcare/income, or a default balanced market, then adjust from all-market share averages. (4) Assign region_growth as gaps vs the global root, starting from APAC +1.0 to +2.0pp, NA -0.5 to 0.0pp, Europe -1.0 to +0.5pp, LATAM -1.0 to -2.0pp, MEA -0.5 to +1.0pp, and shifting premiums upward for infrastructure/regulatory buildout. (5) Split each region into countries using major-country anchors and a residual 'Rest of' line. (6) Assign country_growth gaps using structural tailwinds or constraints, with typical dispersion ±1.3pp. Evidence priority: primary demand/supply data > installed base > income/healthcare spend > manufacturing base > policy/regulation > analyst convention.

- **2A-1** (base_year_anchor, mechanism, 4 markets): when when a hard primary supply/demand anchor exists (regulated quota, official trade data, company product revenue, third-party supply outlook) -> anchor base-year market to that primary source with 0% arbitrary uplift; cross-check with 2–3 independent demand indicators and adjust only if the cross-checks fall outside a plausible side-check range. *Why:* primary data are auditable and reduce top-down error; side checks prevent legal/export overcount, as in DEA quota vs retail prescriptions *Not when:* do NOT apply when the primary data cover only a portion of the market or include non-target products (e.g., broad HS categories); require scope adjustment
- **2A-2** (base_year_anchor, house habit, 1 markets): when when direct sales are unavailable or fragmented -> build a proxy chain from macro spend times narrow adoption shares, e.g., global biopharma R&D × 20–33% discovery/pre-clinical × 1–2% compound handling × 82–88% small molecule; calibrate to historical CAGR and set 2025 as the latest actual year. *Why:* house convention: when direct data are missing, analysts use an economic-proxy chain and tie the result to the observed historical trend to avoid overstating; the 2025 anchor is a standard latest-year convention *Not when:* do NOT use if the proxy share is guessed without a source; require at least one independent validation point before adopting the calibrated level
- **2A-3** (base_year_anchor, house habit, 3 markets): when when market is capex/installed-base driven and current-year shares are available -> anchor base-year to current installed base and regional shares; apply 0% rebase or pandemic catch-up; use historical CAGR only to roll forward. *Why:* installed base represents actual cumulative demand; re-basing one-off shocks would distort the forward view; this is the standard convention when no better primary data exist *Not when:* do NOT apply when the market is in a disruptive phase where new units greatly exceed installed base replacement
- **2A-4** (forecast_growth_vs_history, mechanism, 4 markets): when when market is mature but has one or two structural tailwinds (aging, regulation, sustainability, digitalization) -> set forecast CAGR +0.5 to +0.9pp above history; use all-market mean premium 0.66pp and category means 0.46–0.94pp; do not exceed +1.2pp unless early-growth disruption. *Why:* mature markets absorb new demand gradually; structural drivers lift growth modestly, not a step change *Not when:* do NOT apply if the market is early-growth with a step-change adoption; use 2A-5 instead
- **2A-5** (forecast_growth_vs_history, mechanism, 4 markets): when when market is entering a new adoption phase (AI, digital twin, biologics launch, OT security) -> set forecast premium +0.9 to +1.2pp above history; top of range for software/tech with high historical growth. *Why:* early-growth adoption bends the curve upward; but even hot markets seldom exceed +1.2pp in house data *Not when:* do NOT apply to mature commodity markets or if historical CAGR already >15% and base large
- **2A-6** (forecast_growth_vs_history, mechanism, 1 markets): when when market is saturated, has no new demand driver, or is dominated by replacement/commodity pricing -> set forecast CAGR equal to history or only +0.0 to +0.3pp. *Why:* without new penetration or pricing power, growth cannot exceed observed trend *Not when:* do NOT apply if there is emerging technology substitution or regulatory change; otherwise you will understate
- **2A-7** (region_split, house habit, 3 markets): when when no dominant regional demand driver or starting a new market -> start with all-market average shares: NA 35–38%, Europe 25–28%, APAC 20–24%, LATAM 5–8%, MEA 4–6%; adjust from there. *Why:* house averages from n=37 regional share stats; these are baseline conventions, not universal truth *Not when:* do NOT use as final if industry-specific demand (manufacturing, healthcare, income) differs materially; see 2A-8/2A-9
- **2A-8** (region_split, mechanism, 4 markets): when when demand follows manufacturing/industrial production (chemicals, automation, electronic components) -> upweight APAC to 30–45% and often reduce Europe to 14–24%; NA 28–38%; LATAM 3–7%; MEA 4–7%. *Why:* manufacturing hubs and industrial capex concentrate in Asia Pacific; Europe smaller/import-competitive; NA large but second *Not when:* do NOT apply to income/healthcare-driven markets where US/Europe dominate
- **2A-9** (region_split, mechanism, 4 markets): when when demand is driven by healthcare reimbursement, income, or specialist access (pharma, medical devices, digital services) -> set NA 36–40% (higher for US-centric therapy up to 65%); Europe 25–32%; APAC 18–23%; LATAM 5–8%; MEA 3–6%. *Why:* high-income, high-reimbursement regions concentrate healthcare and digital spend *Not when:* do NOT apply to pure manufacturing markets where APAC often leads
- **2A-10** (region_growth, mechanism, 3 markets): when when regions share common drivers and no extreme infrastructure gap -> set APAC +1.0 to +2.0pp above global; NA -0.5 to 0.0pp; Europe -1.0 to +0.5pp; LATAM -1.0 to -2.0pp; MEA -0.5 to +1.0pp. *Why:* house region gap averages: APAC +1.6pp, NA -0.4pp, Europe -0.8pp, LATAM -1.2pp, MEA -0.8pp *Not when:* adjust for region-specific policy/infrastructure; do not apply blindly to MEA if it has a major capex program
- **2A-11** (region_growth, mechanism, 4 markets): when when region has new infrastructure/capex buildout, regulatory mandates, or rapid digitalization (APAC, MEA, Eastern Europe) -> give positive premium: APAC +1.5 to +3.0pp; MEA +1.0 to +2.5pp; Europe can be +0.5 to +1.5pp if policy-driven; keep NA negative. *Why:* new buildouts add demand above replacement; mandates accelerate adoption *Not when:* do NOT apply when the region's base is already mature and spending is replacement-only
- **2A-12** (country_split, mechanism, 4 markets): when when splitting regions into countries -> use major-country anchors: U.S. 87–89% of NA; Germany 20–25% of Europe; China 30–45% of APAC (higher for industrial, lower for consumer); India 15–20% of APAC; Japan 14–18% of APAC; Brazil 35–50% of LATAM; GCC/Saudi 25–35% of MEA. *Why:* country shares follow population, installed base, and manufacturing/healthcare concentration; these are house central values from country.share stats *Not when:* do NOT use China >50% for consumer/services or <25% for industrial without a strong reason; also do NOT give U.S. <85% of NA unless Canada/Mexico materially share the market
- **2A-13** (country_split, house habit, 3 markets): when when country-level data are thin or region is fragmented -> create a 'Rest of' line with 10–20% of region; use all-market rest_share mean 15.3% as default. *Why:* house convention avoids spurious precision and captures long-tail countries *Not when:* do NOT set rest_share >30% for major economies; if residual is too large, disaggregate further
- **2A-14** (country_growth, mechanism, 3 markets): when when assigning country growth gaps -> set gaps relative to region root; typical absolute gap 0.5–2.0pp; use sd ~1.3pp (stats country.cagr_gap_sd 1.27pp); high-growth emerging +1.0 to +3.0pp; mature/laggard -0.5 to -3.0pp. *Why:* country growth gaps are noisy; house data shows sd 1.3pp, so small differences are common and large gaps need specific evidence *Not when:* do NOT assign more than ±3pp without explicit country event (e.g., sanctions, major policy)
- **2A-15** (country_growth, mechanism, 4 markets): when when country is subject to sanctions, macro instability, or severe policy constraints (Russia, Iran, Argentina, South Africa utility crisis) -> set growth gap -2 to -5pp; possibly -7pp if funding collapses; but do NOT copy extreme outliers like Israel -9.2pp unless you have a specific project cancellation. *Why:* sanctions and constraints directly reduce demand; past analysts used large negative gaps *Not when:* do NOT apply to all emerging markets; only where specific sanctions/macro stress are present
- **2A-16** (country_growth, mechanism, 4 markets): when when country is emerging with low penetration and policy/investment push (India, ASEAN, Saudi, Eastern Europe) -> set growth gap +1.0 to +3.5pp; India typical +1.5 to +2.7pp; ASEAN +0.7 to +2.1pp; Saudi +0.6 to +3.7pp. *Why:* low base, government investment and rising income drive faster growth *Not when:* do NOT apply uniformly; check if country already has high penetration or policy implementation is weak

Pitfalls:
- Copying regional shares from another market without adjusting for demand type (e.g., using APAC-heavy industrial split for US-centric pharma).
- Over-extrapolating historical CAGR into forecast by adding a large premium without evidence; typical premium should be ≤1.2pp unless early-growth disruption.
- Treating MEA and Africa as one region when opinions/stats often separate Africa; ensure consistent region definitions before comparing shares.
- Using extreme country growth gaps from outliers (Russia -5.5pp, Nigeria +7.1pp, Israel -9.2pp) as typical; most gaps are within ±1.3pp.
- Anchoring base year only to one data source without cross-check; e.g., DEA quota alone could overestimate if retail share differs.
- Over-relying on analyst convention '2025 latest year' without re-basing when historical data are poor or the market has a structural break.
- Setting country splits without a residual 'Rest of' for fragmented regions, causing spurious precision and overstating small-country shares.

## 2B - use-case / application split

**Method.** At stage 2B, first decide use_case_split: anchor the largest use case in the most stable structural or regulatory driver (installed base, dominant industry, mandatory use), then set the next 2-4 use cases by volume or value, and leave an 'Other' tail. Next set use_case_growth: take the market root CAGR and add segment growth premiums; premiums should be share-weighted to zero, with most absolute deviations around 0.7-1.2pp and top-to-bottom spread around 2.5-4.5pp. Finally adjust use_case_mix_by_region: start from the global use-case split, then shift regional mixes only where structural industry mix, policy, or channel evidence is strong; keep unexplained or balancing shifts at ±1-3pp. Look for regulation, installed base, infrastructure capex, and channel shift as primary evidence before relying on broad categories like 'population' or 'GDP'.

- **2B-1** (use_case_split, mechanism, 4 markets): when There is a clear regulatory or installed-base anchor, such as metering/grid operations, coastal desalination, hospital settings, or pharma manufacturing. -> Set the dominant use case share at 45-75%; set the second use case at 15-25%; leave a tail of 10-20% across the remaining segments.. *Why:* Installed base and regulatory mandates concentrate demand. Across past segmented markets, seg.top_share averages 52.7%, and anchored examples include coastal desalination 76.3%, general/multispecialty hospitals 72.3%, electric utilities 59.2%, and pharmaceutical companies 56.0%. These are not uniformly distributed. *Not when:* Do not apply when the market has no single regulatory anchor, or when the anchor is intentionally excluded from the market definition.
- **2B-2** (use_case_split, mechanism, 4 markets): when The market spans diverse consumer, industrial, or application segments with no single regulatory or installed-base anchor. -> Set the top use case at 25-35%, the next at 15-25%, and keep an Other tail of 5-15%. Avoid assigning more than 45% to any one segment.. *Why:* Fragmented markets in the past have top shares around 22-34%: Global Adhesives & Bonding packaging 25.8%, Technical Foam automotive 30.8%, Spectral Sensor industrial 29.0%, and Process Analyzer oil/gas 28.4%. The all-market seg.top_share of 52.7% is inflated by anchored markets. *Not when:* If the market itself is defined narrowly around one technology or one procedure, use the anchored heuristic instead.
- **2B-3** (use_case_split, mechanism, 3 markets): when The market includes an emerging technology or early-adoption segment, such as DERMS, cell and gene therapy, agri-tech, or EV charging. -> Assign the emerging segment a small share of 5-15%, not 20-30%, even if it is the fastest-growing; keep the incumbent or core segment as the largest.. *Why:* Early-adoption segments have small current shares. Past examples include EV charging 4.3% in Digital Twin, cell & gene therapy 6.5% in life sciences automation, and agri-tech & smart farming 7.7% in Middle East Agriculture. The share should reflect current installed base, not future promise. *Not when:* If the market definition is itself the emerging technology, the emerging segment can be dominant; do not artificially shrink it.
- **2B-4** (use_case_split, house habit, 3 markets): when There is weak evidence or the analyst has no better structural anchor for the split. -> Default to a house convention of top use case 50-55%, second 20-25%, third 10-15%, and the rest 5-10%.. *Why:* Past analysts often cluster top shares near 50% for diverse medical, retail, or industrial splits: HIFU oncology 52.9%, Athletic Tape amateur athletes 50.6%, and Retail Self Scanning supermarkets/hypermarkets 54.9%. This may be partially mechanical, so treat it only as a prior. *Not when:* Replace this prior as soon as installed base, regulatory, or industry-structure evidence is available.
- **2B-5** (use_case_growth, mechanism, 4 markets): when Regulation or policy explicitly favors a segment, such as renewable fuel mandates, DERMS integration, continuous manufacturing, or bioherbicide substitution. -> Set a growth premium of +1.0 to +3.0pp vs root for the favored segment; if policy is very strong and the segment is still small, use +3.5 to +4.5pp.. *Why:* Past policy-driven premiums include DERMS +3.4pp, continuous manufacturing +3.6pp, renewable energy +2.7pp in process analyzers, and demand response +2.0pp. The all-market seg.cagr_spread average is 2.75pp, with seg.cagr_dev around 0.77pp, so these are upper-tail outliers. *Not when:* Do not apply if the policy is already fully reflected in the base year share or if competing segments do not receive offsetting discounts.
- **2B-6** (use_case_growth, mechanism, 4 markets): when The segment is legacy, mature, or replacement-driven with no new adoption driver, such as ICS security, batch manufacturing, raw water treatment, legacy SCADA, or traditional woodworking. -> Set a growth discount of -0.5 to -2.5pp vs root; for structurally declining segments use -1.5 to -3.5pp.. *Why:* Past mature/declining discounts include ICS -2.5pp, batch manufacturing -1.4pp, raw water treatment -0.6pp, gas utilities -2.6pp, and small molecule pharma -2.2pp. These negative premiums are needed to offset positive premiums and keep the weighted sum near zero. *Not when:* Do not assign every segment a positive premium; that implies you are increasing market growth rather than allocating it.
- **2B-7** (use_case_growth, mechanism, 4 markets): when The segment is an early-stage high-growth niche with small current share, such as EV charging, cell and gene therapy, household/municipal collection, or medical devices within a broader materials market. -> Set a growth premium of +2 to +6pp for very early technology when share is under about 5-10%; if the share is already 10-20% but still fast, use +1 to +3pp.. *Why:* Past examples include EV charging +6.5pp, cell and gene +4.6pp, household/municipal UCO collection +2.7pp, and medical devices in technical foam +1.6pp. The typical seg.cagr_spread is 2.75pp, but early S-curve segments can exceed that; balance with negative premiums elsewhere. *Not when:* Do not apply a +5pp premium if the segment is already large or if several segments each claim +5pp; the sum must be constraint-compatible.
- **2B-8** (use_case_growth, mechanism, 4 markets): when Channel or care is shifting from institutional/professional to home, self-service, DIY, or amateur use. -> Set the self-service/home/DIY channel growth premium at +0.5 to +1.3pp; set the institutional/professional channel discount at -0.3 to -1.2pp.. *Why:* Past examples include Povidone Iodine homecare/LTC +0.8 to +0.9pp vs hospitals +0.1 to +0.2pp and ASCs -1.1 to -1.2pp; Attic Ladder DIY +1.3pp vs professional -1.0pp; and Athletic Tape amateur athletes +0.7pp vs professional -1.2pp. These premiums are consistent with seg.cagr_dev around 0.77pp. *Not when:* If safety regulation or reimbursement strongly favors institutional use, the institutional discount should be smaller or zero.
- **2B-9** (use_case_mix_by_region, mechanism, 3 markets): when A region has a structural industry advantage in a use case, such as APAC electronics/industrial, North America healthcare/defense, Latin America agriculture, or Europe environmental monitoring. -> Shift that region's share of the structural use case +2 to +8pp vs global; offset with negative shifts of -1 to -5pp in less relevant use cases.. *Why:* Past structural shifts include Spectral Sensor healthcare North America +5pp, industrial APAC +5pp, and agriculture Latin America +7pp. Regional size matters: typical global region shares from past estimates are NA 35.8%, Europe 27.0%, APAC 23.0%, LatAm 6.0%; large pp shifts in small regions have limited global impact. *Not when:* Do not shift if the region is import-dependent in that use case or lacks the local manufacturing/service base.
- **2B-10** (use_case_mix_by_region, mechanism, 1 markets): when A small, wealthy policy-driven state is investing heavily in non-conventional or self-sufficiency segments, such as Saudi Vision 2030, Qatar food security, or Kuwait water scarcity. -> Shift strategic segments +5 to +17pp within that state; reduce conventional segments by -10 to -22pp.. *Why:* Past Middle East Agriculture shifts include Kuwait water +17pp and agri-tech +10pp, with crop -22pp; Qatar aquaculture +9pp and agri-tech +6pp, with crop -17pp; Saudi water +5pp and agri-tech +3pp, with crop -8pp. These are extreme but occur in small, high-investment states. *Not when:* Only apply to small GCC-type markets with massive sovereign investment; do not extrapolate to large diversified economies like the U.S., China, or the EU.
- **2B-11** (use_case_mix_by_region, house habit, 4 markets): when No strong local evidence, and the analyst needs to balance global shares or follow past convention. -> Use ±1 to 3pp shifts across regions and use cases; keep most regional changes within ±2pp, especially for large regions.. *Why:* Many past regional mix shifts are small and likely mechanical: Buprenorphine OUD shifts -1pp in Europe/LatAm/MEA; Technical Foam +1pp; Digital Twin DERMS +1pp. The all-market seg.country_mix_sd is about 0.9pp, so small deviations are typical. *Not when:* Do not invent causal stories for every ±1pp; report them as balancing or low-confidence adjustments.
- **2B-12** (use_case_growth, house habit, 3 markets): when The analyst is setting growth premiums for a full set of use cases. -> Ensure the share-weighted sum of premiums equals zero; target typical absolute deviation of 0.7-1.2pp and top-to-bottom spread of 2.5-4.5pp. If all premiums are positive, subtract the weighted average from every premium.. *Why:* Past markets show seg.cagr_dev around 0.77pp and seg.cagr_spread around 2.75pp, meaning most premiums should be modest. North America Electronic Design Services gave all vertical premiums +7 to +12pp, which is mechanically wrong because it implies every segment grows above the root. *Not when:* This is an arithmetic constraint, not a market insight; after centering, still verify that the signs match plausible mechanisms.
- **2B-13** (use_case_mix_by_region, mechanism, 2 markets): when A region is small in global market size, but the use-case shift within that region is large in percentage-point terms. -> Before judging global impact, multiply the regional shift by the region share. Typical region shares are NA 35.8%, Europe 27.0%, APAC 23.0%, LatAm 6.0%, MEA 5.8%.. *Why:* A +15pp shift in Kuwait or Qatar contributes less than 1pp to the global mix if the region is only a few percent of the world market. Past examples like Kuwait water +17pp or Qatar aquaculture +9pp should be interpreted within the Middle East Agriculture market, not as global shifts. *Not when:* If the market is defined only for that region, such as a Latin America bone cement market or Saudi kitchen market, this global weighting does not apply.

Pitfalls:
- Avoid copying mechanical 'top share = 50%' splits; use installed-base or regulatory anchors when available.
- Do not set all use-case growth premiums positive; they must be share-weighted to zero and mostly within ±2.5pp.
- Do not over-interpret small ±1pp regional mix shifts; many are balancing conventions, not real demand signals.
- Do not apply large GCC-style policy shifts to large diversified economies.
- Do not confuse a high growth premium with a large current share; emerging segments should often remain small.
- Do not ignore region size: a +10pp shift in LatAm (~6% global) is smaller than a +2pp shift in NA (~36%) for global mix.
- Be skeptical of analyses where every segment grows above the root, such as NA Electronic Design Services, and normalize before using.

## 2C - product types inside use cases, sub-segments, price by region

**Method.** At stage 2C, form opinions in this order: (1) identify the primary type dimensions—product type, technology, service model, deployment, grade—and classify what is the default/standard vs the emerging/premium type; (2) set global type_split shares for 2025 using installed base and standard-of-care, not equal splits or forced 50% anchors; (3) set type_growth premiums for each type relative to the market root using technology adoption, regulation/sustainability, brand vs generic, services/software shift, and convenience/channel shift; (4) for the most important parent types, set sub_segment_split shares and growth using application pain-points and maturity; (5) adjust type_mix_by_region only where regional maturity, industrial base, or cost sensitivity differ, keeping shifts zero-sum; (6) set price_by_region indices using income, premium mix, and commodity vs specialty. Evidence to look for first: installed base and standard-of-care, technology adoption curve stage, regulatory/policy direction, regional end-use/industrial composition, and payer/consumer preferences. Do not copy common flat-region or 50%-top-share anchors without checking them.

- **2C-1** (type_split, house habit, 4 markets): when A market has a clear default/standard product type or technology used by most buyers -> Set the default type's share at 50-65% if there are 2-4 types, or 30-50% if there are 5+ types; set the rest by application niche and price tier.. *Why:* Past estimates cluster top type share around 50-58% (all seg.top_share mean 52.7%, n=58). Markets with established standards such as gas analyzers 58%, traditional round pizza 52%, and PU foam 45% show concentration. This is a robust house anchor but must be adjusted by installed base and standard-of-care. *Not when:* Do not apply to multi-technology markets with no standard or to premium niches like beam-combined lasers (26%) or fiber lasers (35%).
- **2C-2** (type_split, mechanism, 4 markets): when One type is legacy/installed-base and another is new/premium but small -> Set legacy share 50-80% and emerging share 5-20%; do not give emerging equal share yet.. *Why:* Current revenue follows installed base: fixed kiosks 87%, barcode scanning 86%, PMMA cement 94%, solvent-based decaf 64%, MCUs 46%. Emerging technologies remain small until replacement or regulatory drivers mature. *Not when:* If market is already past early adoption (e.g., cloud in head-end at 42%), emerging share can exceed 30%.
- **2C-3** (type_growth, mechanism, 5 markets): when A type benefits from adoption of new technology or performance shift -> Set growth premium +1 to +4 pp vs root for the emerging/performing type; set mature legacy type -0.5 to -2 pp. If emerging type is <5% share, use +3 to +6 pp but cap extremes.. *Why:* Typical segment growth spread is 2.75pp (all seg.cagr_spread, n=58). Examples: hyperspectral imaging +2.9, cybersecurity services +1.9, cloud deployment +3.0, AI coaching +2.7, fiber lasers +1.7. Legacy types fall below root. *Not when:* Do not copy near-zero base extremes like Phase 3 drugs +42.7pp; renormalize to market impact.
- **2C-4** (type_growth, mechanism, 4 markets): when Regulation or sustainability shifts demand toward compliant types -> Give compliant/sustainable type +0.5 to +1.5 pp growth premium; non-compliant/solvent/legacy type -0.5 to -1.5 pp. For strong policy penalties, sustainable type can be +2 to +3 pp.. *Why:* Examples: bio-based foam +2.8, radiation-cured adhesive +2.6, solvent-based adhesive -1.2. Regulatory shifts are a recurring mechanism with typical segment spread 2.6-3.0pp in chemicals (Chemicals seg.cagr_spread 0.026). *Not when:* If no regulation or substitute exists, tailwinds may be premature; avoid applying to all eco-labels equally.
- **2C-5** (type_growth, mechanism, 4 markets): when Split involves branded/premium vs generic/commodity versions -> Set branded/premium growth +0.3 to +0.6 pp above root; generic/commodity -0.2 to -1.0 pp. If generics face severe price erosion, generic can be -1.5 to -2.5 pp.. *Why:* Brand retains innovation or payer preference: buprenorphine branded +0.4 vs generic -0.9; CSU biologic branded +0.5 vs generic -2.3. Pharma typical segment spread is 2.1pp. *Not when:* If brand faces patent cliff or severe formulary exclusion, set brand negative.
- **2C-6** (type_growth, mechanism, 3 markets): when Split includes services/software vs hardware -> Set services/software +0.9 to +3.0 pp vs root; hardware -0.6 to -1.5 pp. Software often +2 to +3 pp if analytics/SaaS; services +0.9 to +1.9 pp.. *Why:* After installed base build-out, spend shifts to software and recurring services: cyber services +1.9, head-end services +0.9/software +0.1, life-science software +2.2/hardware -1.1. ICT segment spread 4.5pp supports wider software-service gaps. *Not when:* If hardware replacement supercycle or regulatory-driven hardware upgrade occurs (e.g., AMI, defense), hardware can be above root.
- **2C-7** (type_growth, mechanism, 3 markets): when Convenience or channel shift favors a new delivery/format -> Give new convenience/digital format +1.5 to +4 pp vs root; traditional format -0.5 to -2 pp.. *Why:* Pods/capsules +2.7, mobile self-scanning +4.9, self-paced digital +1.6. Consumers/enterprises shift to seamless/remote formats while legacy channels lose share. *Not when:* If the new channel remains niche or requires expensive hardware, growth premium may be lower or negative.
- **2C-8** (type_mix_by_region, mechanism, 3 markets): when Regions differ in adoption maturity or infrastructure stage -> Shift advanced/premium type share up +2 to +5 pp in early-adopter/developed regions; shift basic/build-out type share up +2 to +5 pp in emerging/late-adopter regions. Counter-shift other regions, keeping zero-sum.. *Why:* Head-end system: APAC hardware +3, MEA +5, NA software +2; spectral sensor: LatAm multispectral +5, MEA +2; digital twin: MEA/LatAm asset +1. Adoption curves drive mix by installed base. *Not when:* Do not use if regions have similar maturity; then shifts should be small or zero.
- **2C-9** (type_mix_by_region, mechanism, 3 markets): when Regional industrial base or regulatory regime differs -> Set type share shift ±1 to ±3 pp in specific regions, offset elsewhere. Use +2/-2 for strong industrial or regulatory differences; +1/-1 for mild differences.. *Why:* Technical foam: Europe PU +3, NA high-performance +1; process analyzer: Europe gas +1, MEA +1; azelaic acid: NA pharma +2, Europe cosmetic +1. Regional end-use/regulatory orientation drives product mix. *Not when:* Do not set arbitrary non-zero shifts; they should be tied to a concrete end-use/regulatory difference.
- **2C-10** (type_mix_by_region, mechanism, 2 markets): when Emerging/cost-sensitive regions have weaker infrastructure or payer budgets -> Shift basic/cost-effective type share up +1 to +3 pp in emerging regions; shift premium type up +1 to +3 pp in developed regions, offset zero-sum.. *Why:* Gel ice pack: disposable +2 LatAm, +1 MEA; collagen membrane: non-crosslinked +2 Ecuador/El Salvador, Brazil -1 toward cost. Income and infrastructure drive mix. *Not when:* Not all emerging regions use basic types; APAC often adopts premium/reusable quickly.
- **2C-11** (sub_segment_split, mechanism, 3 markets): when A parent segment contains multiple applications/technologies -> Set largest subsegment to 25-60% of parent if multiple; set fastest subsegment growth +1 to +3 pp vs parent, slowest legacy -1 to -3 pp. Use installed base for share, adoption for growth.. *Why:* Process analyzer gas subsegments: industrial gases 24%, emission 18%, hydrogen +3.0 vs industrial gases -1.5. High energy laser: counter-UAS 32%, +2.6 vs guidance -2.2. Sub-segment typical deviation is 0.88pp (all seg.sub_cagr_dev) but ranges to ±3pp. *Not when:* If subsegments are very fragmented, largest can be <25% and growth may vary outside ±3pp.
- **2C-12** (sub_segment_split, mechanism, 3 markets): when A subsegment is small (<10% share) and emerging, or mature and commodity -> Set small emerging subsegment growth +2 to +5 pp vs parent; mature/commodity subsegment -0.5 to -2 pp. Set larger mature subsegments near parent growth.. *Why:* Hydrogen in gas analyzers 9% +3.0, biomethane 5% +2.1; CV/AI 10.7% +5.7 in retail scanning; gummies 1.5% +4.0; traditional industrial gases -1.5. *Not when:* Cap small-base extremes; do not let tiny segments distort market growth.
- **2C-13** (price_by_region, mechanism, 3 markets): when Product is premium/technology-intensive and regions differ in income/purchasing power -> Set North America/Europe price index 1.0-1.25, Asia Pacific/Latin America 0.75-0.95, MEA 0.9-1.1 (global=1.0). Use higher NA premium for specialty/consumer products.. *Why:* Decaf coffee: NA 1.25, Europe 1.04, APAC 0.75; UCO: NA 1.15, Africa 0.72; process analyzer: NA 1.05, APAC 0.95. Income, premium mix, and logistics produce these ranges. *Not when:* If product is undifferentiated, use parity range in 2C-14.
- **2C-14** (price_by_region, mechanism, 3 markets): when Product is commodity/logistics-heavy with low differentiation -> Set regional price indices close to 1.0 (±0.03); remote/import-dependent regions +0.05 to +0.15; low-income regions 0.95-1.00 if no premium mix.. *Why:* Bone cement: Brazil 1.01, Argentina 1.01, Colombia 0.99; collagen membranes: Brazil 1.02, El Salvador 0.96; India SWRO: Northeast 1.13 vs South 0.99. Small deviations reflect logistics, duties, local competition. *Not when:* Even commodity products can have large regional gaps if raw material/logistics or policy demand differ.
- **2C-15** (type_mix_by_region, house habit, 1 markets): when No clear regional driver is identified for type mix -> Set regional type-mix shifts to zero (flat); this is a house convention to avoid spurious differences only after checking installed base, industrial mix, and regulatory regimes.. *Why:* Retail self-scanning held product-type mix flat across regions because the analyst saw no material difference; many past analysts also default to zero. This is acceptable when markets are homogeneous, but it is often overused. *Not when:* Do not apply zero mechanically; if any region has different maturity, income, or industrial base, use 2C-8/2C-10.

Pitfalls:
- Mechanical zero regional type shifts: many past analysts set type_mix_by_region flat without evidence (e.g., product type in retail self-scanning); only use zero if regional markets are truly homogeneous.
- Overusing extreme growth premiums for near-zero bases: Phase 3 drugs +42.7pp is an analyst artifact, not a market lever; cap small-base premiums to +3 to +6pp unless explicitly renormalizing.
- Setting every type growth to root: past opinion lists often include many ±0pp or tiny shifts; use the segment spread mean 2.75pp to ensure meaningful differentiation.
- Ignoring brand vs generic when setting type_growth: branded/premium types usually deserve +0.3 to +0.6pp and generic/commodity -0.2 to -1.0pp; flat brand/generic growth is unrealistic.
- Forcing 50-60% top share: in fragmented/technology-transition markets top share can be 25-40% (fiber lasers 35%, beam combined 26%); do not anchor all type splits to 50%.
- Applying developed-market price premiums to commodity products: bone cement and collagen membrane should be near parity; only premium, income-sensitive products get NA/EU 1.15-1.25.
- Neglecting regional zero-sum when shifting type_mix_by_region: every positive shift must be offset across regions to maintain global share.

## Calibration notes (from the mimic test)

- **CAL-1** (all): Market-specific evidence (installed base, OEM/shipment data, trade data, company revenues) overrides every house default band. A house_convention band is the weakest input: use it only when nothing specific is known, and say so.
- **CAL-2** (region_split): Region-share bands (2A-7/8/9) describe the standard five-region global split (NA, Europe, APAC, LATAM, MEA). For any other split (e.g. U.S. / China / Europe, or sub-national regions) do not use them; reason from each geography's own consumption.
- **CAL-3** (region_split): For aftermarket, service, spare-part, repair or consumable markets, the base-year split follows the INSTALLED BASE where the assets already are (cumulative past installations and their age) - not where new capacity or manufacturing is growing today. Fast new-build regions earn a growth premium, not an inflated base-year share. Choose the heuristic whose CONDITION matches the market's demand mechanism before applying its numbers.

Source: mimic test 2026-11-10: held-out Glass Packaging (CAL-2) and the gearbox (CAL-3, so the gearbox is not fully blind on this point) showed heuristics applied outside their conditions

## Disabled in use

- region_split heuristics (2A-7/8/9) are NOT given to analysts: mimic test 2026-11-10: with the region-share bands the analyst's base-year geography shares got worse on held-out markets (2.86 -> 3.94 pp; calibrated 3.66 pp) and on the gearbox (3.19 -> 7.00 pp), while every other decision improved; region shares are left to market evidence (OEM installed base), growth gaps keep their heuristics.
